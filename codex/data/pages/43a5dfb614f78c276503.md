 Behavioral Threat 2.0: Rebooting Insider Risk Management 

 Release Date: July 2026 
 | 
 Last Updated: July 2026 

 Introduction 

 UEBA 2.0 represents a complete restructuring of User and Entity Behavior Analytics to deliver a fundamentally enhanced insider risk management. Built on six foundational pillars—Data Source Expansion, User Investigation, Policy Customization, Monitoring, Enforcement Controls, and Traceability—UEBA 2.0 empowers security teams and executives to identify, investigate, and respond to insider threats with unprecedented transparency and control. 

 UEBA 2.0 moves from traditional ML-only systems to fully explainable risk scoring, comprehensive user activity timelines, and real-time enforcement integration. 

 Data Source Expansion: Multi-Channel DLP Aggregation 

 You can now detect risky user behavior across all data loss prevention channels through a multi-channel DLP aggregation policy that correlates violations at the user level and creates Behavior Threats incidents. This policy monitors DLP violations across six enforcement channels—Endpoint DLP, NGFW, Email DLP, Prisma® Access, SaaS API, and Prisma® Access Browser—to identify users who exceed a configurable threshold of violations within a single day. 

 When a user triggers the threshold (default: 5 violations), the platform creates a Behavior Threats incident that aggregates all contributing DLP violations with direct links to the Unified Incident Manager for detailed investigation. You can configure which channels to monitor, the severity filter, enforcement action filter, incident threshold, and data profiles to exclude. The policy participates in risk score calculation based on its assigned policy weight. 

 This multi-channel approach ensures that data exfiltration attempts across any vector are captured and correlated, providing comprehensive visibility into user behavior and enabling faster incident response. For more information, refer to the Behavior Threats DLP Violations Policy documentation . 

 User Investigation: Transparent Risk Scoring and Activity Timelines 

 UEBA 2.0 transforms user investigation through two capabilities: a fully explainable risk scoring model and a comprehensive User Activity Timeline that shows exactly the breakdown of user incidents and risk contribution over time. 

 Transparent Risk Scoring : You can now understand exactly how user risk scores are calculated through a fully explainable, transparent risk scoring model. Behavior Threats assigns a risk score to each user based on default weights set for each policy, count of incident, and severity of the breach. Higher weights mean higher impact on the user's risk score. You have full control over the scoring model by editing weights (1 to 10) for both the ML baseline and each static policy. The platform compiles the score directly from your admin-configured weights, ensuring complete transparency. This allows you to tune the system to match your organization's unique risk appetite. 

 The platform maps scores to five severity levels: Very Low (0-40), Low (41-60), Medium (61-80), High (81-90), and Critical (91-100). Smart decay logic automatically reduces the impact of older incidents based on recency—incidents less than 7 days old carry full weight, while those between 7-30 days and up to 90 days carry progressively less. This prevents stale data from inflating risk scores and triggering false positives. 

 User Activity Timeline : You can now view a complete chronological record of user policy violations and their individual risk contributions through the User Activity Timeline. This timeline provides full transparency into how a user's risk score builds over time, showing each policy violation, its severity, and the exact percentage it contributed to the overall risk score. The User Activity Timeline displays all policy violations in reverse chronological order, allowing you to trace the progression of risky behavior and understand the cumulative impact of incidents. Each entry shows the date, policy violated, detection rule triggered, severity level, and risk contribution percentage. This detailed view enables security teams to quickly identify patterns, assess the severity of user behavior, and make informed decisions about enforcement actions. 

 By understanding exactly which policies triggered and how much each violation contributed to the risk score, you can confidently investigate incidents and take appropriate remediation steps. The timeline is essential for compliance audits and stakeholder reporting, providing clear evidence of the risk assessment process. For more information, refer to the Behavior Threats Transparent Risk Scoring documentation and User Activity Timeline documentation . 

 Policy Customization: Tuning Risk Scoring to Your Organization 

 Security teams often struggle to trust traditional machine learning-based UEBA systems because the risk scoring models lack transparency and granular control. Customizable Policy Weights in Behavior Threats addresses this challenge by introducing a transparent, fully explainable risk-scoring model that empowers you to align user risk assessment with your organization's unique risk appetite. 

 Behavior Threats assigns default weights to each policy and the ML baseline. You can now configure and assign specific weights (1 to 10) to both the ML baseline and each individual static policy to ensure the scoring accurately reflects your business outcomes. The platform compiles the score directly from your admin-configured weights, providing complete transparency. If you do not require customization, the system operates using predefined default weights. 

 This transparent approach enables you to confidently tune out background noise and drastically reduce false positives. Coupled with smart decay logic that automatically reduces the weight of incidents based on recency, your security operations center (SOC) analysts can view a user's activity timeline and identify the exact percentage each policy violation contributed to the overall score. This clear explainability ensures you always understand exactly why a user was flagged for suspicious behavior. For more information on how to adjust policy weights, refer to the Behavior Threats Customizable Policy Weights documentation . 

 Monitoring: Targeted Watchlists with Risk Amplifiers 

 You can now create and manage targeted watchlists to group and monitor high-risk users with configurable risk amplifiers that elevate their risk scores proportionally. Behavior Threats provides four predefined watchlists—Departing Users, High Exec, Vendors, and High Risk Users—and allows you to create up to six additional custom watchlists tailored to your organization, for a total of ten watchlists per tenant. 

 Each watchlist includes a configurable risk amplifier that multiplies the impact of policy violations for users in that group, ensuring that threats from high-priority personas surface faster during triage. You can enable, disable, or edit any predefined watchlist, and perform full create, edit, and delete operations on custom watchlists. This flexibility enables you to adapt your monitoring strategy to match your organization's evolving risk landscape. 

 When a user belongs to multiple watchlists, the system automatically applies the highest risk amplifier among all their group memberships. This ensures that users with the most critical risk profile receive the appropriate level of scrutiny. All watchlist actions—including adding or removing users—are recorded in the audit logs for compliance tracking. For more information on how to configure watchlists and assign risk amplifiers, refer to the Behavior Threats Watchlists documentation and Risk Amplifier documentation . 

 Enforcement Controls: Real-Time Access Management Integration 

 You can now view and manage Cloud Dynamic User Group (CDUG) membership directly from a user's detail page to tightly couple risk scores with real-time access enforcement through Cloud Identity Engine (CIE). Behavior Threats displays all CDUGs associated with a user, giving you immediate context about which enforcement policies currently apply based on their risk level—without switching to a separate interface. 

 When you determine a user no longer poses a threat and reset their risk score, the platform automatically removes them from their associated CDUG in near real-time, restoring access without requiring manual group management in CIE. When a user's risk score is reset, it is set to 5—which may move them out of the CDUG since the filter condition may not be met—thus helping in enforcement in real time and restoring their access instantaneously. For users who are not yet part of a CDUG-based enforcement flow, use the Manage CDUG option to be redirected to CIE. From CIE, you can add a risk connection for Behavior Threats. After the risk connection is established, you can create risky user groups. This automated enforcement loop ensures elevated risk triggers immediate restrictions while score resets lift them instantaneously. 

 For executives, this integration delivers measurable risk reduction through automated enforcement—threats are contained immediately upon detection, maintaining the ability to restore access when investigations conclude. For more information on CDUG visibility and enforcement, refer to the Behavior Threats Dynamic User Group Integration documentation . 

 Traceability: Complete Audit Logging for Compliance 

 Security teams require complete traceability of administrative actions to maintain accountability and meet strict compliance standards. Audit logging in Behavior Threats automatically captures and records all critical administrative workflow events, ensuring full traceability for auditors by tracking exactly who performed an action, what the action entailed, and when it occurred. 

 The system logs the following administrative events: 

 User Risk Management: Resetting a user's risk score. 

 Watchlist Management: Creating, modifying, or deleting custom watchlists, and adding or removing users from watchlists. 

 Policy Configuration: Adjusting static policy weights or toggling policies on or off. 

 Reporting Management: Downloading reports. 

 All audit logs include timestamps, user identities, action descriptions, and justification comments (where applicable), providing complete visibility into administrative activities for compliance audits and incident investigations. For executives, comprehensive audit logging demonstrates governance and accountability, supporting regulatory compliance and internal audit requirements. For more information on how to access and interpret these logs, refer to the Behavior Threats Audit Logging documentation .

 Centralized Audit Logging for SaaS Security Inline 

 Release Date: July 2026 
 | 
 Last Updated: July 2026 

 You can now monitor all SaaS Security Inline administrative actions from the central audit logs on Strata Cloud Manager, eliminating the need to track administrator activity across separate interfaces. Previously, audit logs for SaaS Security Inline were only available within the SaaS Security settings page, making it difficult to correlate administrator actions across services managed by Strata Cloud Manager . 

 SaaS Security Inline now publishes audit events for all state-changing operations to the centralized audit logging system on Strata Cloud Manager . Logged actions include creating, updating, and deleting security policy rules, managing custom tags and application sanctioned status, updating risk score configurations, generating reports, and exporting data. Read-only operations such as viewing dashboards or searching applications are not logged. 

 Each audit log entry captures who performed the action, what the action was, and when it occurred. You can view these events alongside audit logs from other services by navigating to the Log Viewer on Strata Cloud Manager .

 Enhanced Bitbucket Onboarding in Data Security via Atlassian Forge 

 Release Date: July 2026 
 | 
 Last Updated: July 2026 

 Following Atlassian’s formal deprecation of the Atlassian Connect framework, Palo Alto Networks has modernized the Bitbucket cloud integration process within Data Security . We have updated our onboarding workflow to utilize Atlassian's next-generation Forge app framework, combined with the Workspace Universally Unique Identifier (UUID) retrieved directly from Bitbucket. 

 Key Benefits of the New Onboarding Procedure 

 Elevated Security & Trust: Building on the native Atlassian Forge framework ensures that authorization and data processing adhere to modern cloud governance and strict data isolation boundaries. 

 Streamlined Workspace Identification: Utilizing the Bitbucket workspace UUID eliminates configuration ambiguities, delivering a more reliable and precise connection during initial setup. 

 Future-Proof Integration: Transitioning away from legacy Connect architecture guarantees long-term compliance with Atlassian’s roadmap, ensuring uninterrupted data security scanning and support. 

 To maintain seamless visibility and protection over your repositories, transition your existing integrations or onboard new instances using the updated Forge app procedure outlined in the technical documentation .

 Enhanced Content Cloud Analysis Transport Support 

 Release Date: July 2026 
 | 
 Last Updated: August 2026 

 Palo Alto Networks introduces a redesigned transport architecture for cloud-delivered security services that eliminates performance bottlenecks in the Multi Inline Cloud Analysis (MICA) data forwarding channel. This architecture improves inline cloud analysis for Enterprise DLP , Advanced Threat Prevention , Advanced URL Filtering , Advanced WildFire , Prisma AIRS (AI Runtime Security), and ACE (App-ID Cloud Engine) when used alongside SaaS Security Inline , and AI Access Security . 

 The new architecture removes the fixed-core transport limitation that previously constrained cloud submission throughput regardless of available hardware resources. All data plane cores now natively handle payload forwarding to the cloud through a scalable connection pool. Each connection is established directly from the data plane over TLS, eliminating the intermediate process that previously serialized all cloud submissions through a single core. 

 The redesigned transport reduces latency by replacing protocol translation layers with a direct binary protocol between the data plane and advanced service addresses. Native PAN-OS memory pools replace the previously fixed-size memory allocation, reducing dedicated memory consumption and increasing overall system capacity. The newly introduced Advanced Forwarding discovery service dynamically assigns advanced service addresses based on your NGFW's location and configuration, improving reliability and enabling automatic failover without manual intervention. 

 When Advanced Forwarding is enabled, the NGFW establishes TLS connections directly from the data plane to the cloud. To use the legacy transport instead of Advanced Forwarding, you must manually disable Advanced Forwarding. The NGFW does not automatically fall back to the legacy transport method.

 SaaS Security Inline RBAC Support 

 Release Date: July 2026 
 | 
 Last Updated: July 2026 

 SaaS Security Inline permissions are now integrated into the Strata Cloud Manager Role-Based Access Control (RBAC) framework. This update allows you to delegate specific administrative tasks for SaaS Security Inline while maintaining the principle of least privilege. 

 A new SaaS Inline Administrator predefined role provides full read and write access dedicated specifically to SaaS Security Inline features. Additionally, existing roles now include the new permissions. Users assigned to the predefined Superuser role continue to have full control over SaaS Security Inline, while View Only Administrators maintain comprehensive read-only access. 

 You can now tailor custom roles to allow granular access to SaaS Security Inline features based on administrative needs. For each functional area, you can assign full read and write access, read-only access, or no access at all. This flexibility helps you ensure that users can perform only the tasks necessary for their job. For example, a role might allow users to monitor discovered apps and generate reports without being able to modify risk scores or submit policy recommendations. 

 You can view the permissions assigned to predefined roles, and assign permissions to custom roles from the Identity & Access page in Strata Cloud Manager .
