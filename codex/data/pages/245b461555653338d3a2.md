This topic describes how to get information and insights on secrets and secret stores.

View scanned secrets

All the secrets that you have discovered from the secret stores that you've scanned are available in the Secrets page.

The secrets table includes information on each secret. Click the secret in the table to display more details.

Filter and view secrets managed by AWS

When AWS manages secret rotation, the secret is listed on the Secrets page.

To view rotation details:

Select the secret to open the Details pane.

Click the Rotation settings tab.

The tab shows rotation settings retrieved from AWS. Only fields that AWS returns with values appear in the Rotation settings tab.

This tab is available only when AWS manages the secret.

Onboard AWS-managed secrets to Secrets Hub

To onboard secrets that are currently rotated and managed by AWS, do the following:

In the AWS console, open the secret in AWS Secrets Manager and turn off automatic rotation.

In Secrets Hub, scan the relevant secret store again.

Onboard the secret. For details, see Onboard unmanaged secrets to PAM via Secrets Hub.

Insights

You can find the insights at the top of the Secrets page.

Insights are filters that provide you with extra information on your secrets calculated by Secrets Hub.

The following table describes each insight.

Insight

	

Description




Newly scanned secrets

	

New secrets that have been discovered in your secret stores in the last 90 days.

This insight helps you focus on secrets that were scanned recently and may need your attention.

The newly scanned secrets are not the total secrets scanned. If the last secret store was scanned 90 days ago, the newly scanned secrets will be zero (0).




Multi-version secrets

(GCP and HashiCorp only)

	

Secrets that have more than one enabled version.




Idle secrets

(AWS only)

	

Secrets that have not been retrieved in the last 90 days.

This insight helps you identify unused secrets so you can reduce the security risks they pose. Keeping unused secrets in production unnecessarily expands the attack surface and complicates secret management and compliance.

Removing idle secrets can also reduce storage costs. If a secret is unmanaged, you can delete it directly.




Onboard candidates

	

Scanned secrets that are sourced from an external secret store, such as AWS Secrets Manager and not from Idira PAM.

This insight helps you determine which secrets you want to onboard and manage in Idira PAM.




Expired secrets (GCP and Azure only)

	

Secrets that expired in the last 90 days.

This insight helps you determine which secrets are no longer used and can be removed from the secret store.

Leaving expired secrets in production creates a false sense of security, increases the risk of reactivation or exploitation, and may lead to compliance and audit failures. You can delete these secrets if they are unmanaged.




Disabled secrets (GCP and Azure only)

	

Secrets that have been disabled in the last 90 days. These secrets do not have a single enabled version.

This insight helps you identify secrets that should be deleted. You can delete these secrets if they are unmanaged. Leaving disabled secrets in production increases the risk of accidental reactivation, unauthorized access, compliance violations, and exploitation by attackers or insiders.

Delete unmanaged secrets

You can delete unmanaged secrets from the Secrets page.

Delete a secret that is not synced

On the Secrets page, locate the relevant secret that you want to delete.

Click More options and select Delete from target.

Click Delete to confirm.

Delete multiple secrets that are not synced

On the Secrets page, filter for the secrets that you want to delete. Use an insight or other filter type.

Select the checkbox at the top of the list to select all secrets, or select the checkbox of individual secrets.

Click Delete.

Click Delete to confirm.

Secret transformations

A secret transformation defines how a secret is structured and formatted before it’s synced from Idira PAM to a cloud platform like AWS, GCP, or Azure. It controls the shape of the secret value — for example, mapping PAM account fields (username, password, address, etc.) into a target-specific format.

Secret transformations help ensure that secrets are delivered in the correct format, with the right metadata (tags) and naming conventions, to match what the target system expects.
