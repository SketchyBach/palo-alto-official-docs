 Workspaces Overview 

 A workspace is a named subset of your tenant's resources. Certificates, issuing templates, providers, and notification rules each belong either to a workspace or to the tenant as a whole, and the workspace you have selected decides which of them you see. 

 Why Use Workspaces 

 Without workspaces, everyone who can sign in to Next-Gen Trust Security works against the whole tenant. Workspaces let you give a team the certificates and configuration it owns without giving it everything else. A team that manages certificates for one business unit sees that business unit's inventory, and a workspace administrator for one workspace has no visibility into another. 

 Access to a workspace comes from an IAM scope in your identity provider, not from a setting in Next-Gen Trust Security. Creating a workspace also creates its scope. Assigning that scope to a user or a service account is what grants access, so workspace membership is managed alongside the rest of your identity configuration. 

 If You Already Use Tenants and Sub-Tenants 

 If your organization has already activated Next-Gen Trust Security, the tenant you activated it in is what the switcher calls Tenant . Nothing you configured there moves, and your certificate authorities, VSatellites, and inventory are where you left them. 

 A workspace does what a sub-tenant did, without what made a sub-tenant expensive. Creating one needs no permission to create a tenant, and no sharing of products or credits to make it usable. Workspaces are flat, so there is no hierarchy to keep in step with anything else. 

 Sub-tenants still have uses that a workspace does not cover, such as running Next-Gen Trust Security in a separate environment or in a separate region. Those remain separate tenants, each with its own activation. 

 Tenant Is Not a Workspace 

 Every tenant has a default selection labelled Tenant . It represents the whole tenant rather than a subset of it, so it has no scope, no members, and it does not appear in the workspaces inventory. It appears in the workspace switcher, and it is what you have selected when no workspace is selected. 

 Two consequences are worth knowing before you use the feature: 

 A column headed Workspace can hold the value Tenant . That is correct, and it means the resource belongs to the tenant rather than to any workspace. 

 Selecting Tenant shows resources from every workspace, and adds a Workspace column so you can tell them apart. Selecting any other workspace shows only that workspace's resources, and drops the column: every row belongs to the workspace you are already in, so it has nothing left to tell you. 

 Certificates are the exception, in two ways. A certificate has three states rather than two — unclaimed, assigned to the tenant, or assigned to a workspace — and the Certificates page keeps its Workspace column in every workspace, because an unclaimed certificate can show up anywhere. 

 Sharing Resources Between Workspaces 

 A resource is owned by one workspace, but it does not have to be visible only there. Sharing a resource with another workspace gives that workspace read-only access to it, exactly as sharing between sub-tenants did. 

 Sharing a machine also shares what that machine finds. If an F5 is shared between two workspaces, a certificate discovered on that machine appears in both, without either workspace claiming it. Workspaces are flat, so sharing is always between two workspaces in the same tenant. There is no sharing across tenants. 

 Not everything can be shared, and not everything belongs to a workspace in the first place. Workspace-Scoped Resources lists which resources are which. 

 What Workspaces Don't Do 

 Workspaces control which resources you see, not which pages you can open. Permissions and roles control pages. If you want to restrict the pages a user can reach, create a custom role — a workspace is the wrong mechanism, and using it that way won't hold. 

 You also cannot nest a workspace inside another, or delete one once you have created it. Nothing assigns resources to a workspace for you: a certificate found by network discovery arrives unclaimed at the tenant level and stays there until somebody claims it into a workspace. Some pages behave differently outside the tenant selection, described in Workspace Availability by Page . 

 There is also no view that spans several workspaces. You have one workspace selected at a time, and Tenant is the only selection that shows resources from all of them. Tenant is available only to users with tenant-level access, so somebody whose access comes entirely from workspace scopes works in one workspace at a time and switches between them, even when they belong to several. If a user needs to see everything they are responsible for in a single list, give them tenant-level access — dividing those resources across workspaces is precisely what stops them appearing together.
